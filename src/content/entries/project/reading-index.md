A reading index brings saved material and the thoughts around it into one place. The aim is to make an interesting source easy to find again, with enough context to pick up the thread.

![A reading index with topics, saved articles, and a note open beside the list.](/images/reading-index-preview.svg)

## Save a useful fragment

When a passage stands out, save the link and a sentence about why it matters. Connect it to a topic that is already taking shape.

That small amount of context helps the material become useful on a later visit.

## Give each note a shape

A note holds a title, source, topic, brief interpretation, and something to try. Each field supports either finding the source again or carrying the idea forward.

Topics offer an entry point. New connections can emerge as a subject becomes more familiar.

## Keep the next step visible

The list shows titles and topics. Selecting an item opens its note. The source stays beside the title, and a next step sits at the end of the note.

After trying an idea, add a short account of what happened. Reading and making begin to form a connected record.

## Try one familiar task

Start with a small set of material and one task: find an old article and continue the thought that led to saving it.

Watch which clues make the search easier, then refine the relationship between the list, topics, and notes.
