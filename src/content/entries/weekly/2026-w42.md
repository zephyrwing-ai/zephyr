Once an idea becomes a page, vague questions become concrete. A prototype gives a conversation something to refer to: a layout, a piece of content, a sequence of actions.

## Find the main path

For a reading site, the path is simple: enter a section, find an article, and move to a passage worth reading.

Following that path gives each part of the page a purpose. Navigation helps with finding. The article holds the substance. The outline offers a sense of place.

## Design with content

A long title, several paragraphs, and an image quickly reveal what a layout needs to handle. Titles wrap. Images have proportions. Outlines grow as sections are added.

Content with realistic lengths brings these changes into view early.

## Keep the reasoning

After a change, leave a sentence about why it helped. A narrower column may make long passages easier to read. A collapsed outline may give an article more room on a phone.

These short notes keep later decisions connected to the original intent.
