Some things are worth keeping: a passage from a book, a street walked slowly, an idea that finally begins to take shape. A weekly note gives these moments a place to settle.

## Start with a question

Why does this interface feel comfortable? How does a building bring light into a corridor? What would be useful to learn from the next prototype?

Write down the question, then collect a few observations around it. A clear question gives the next piece of reading or experiment a direction.

## Leave a way back

A useful reading note holds three things: the source, an interpretation, and something to try.

> A good note leaves a path for a future visit.

A link preserves the source. A short explanation makes the idea clearer. An action brings it into everyday life. One sentence for each is often enough.

### A small note

- What stood out: one detail worth remembering.
- Why it matters: its connection to the current question.
- What comes next: a small experiment to carry it further.

## Make the experiment concrete

Before starting, decide what the experiment should answer. An article page, for example, can help explore heading hierarchy, line length, and the usefulness of a table of contents.

A specific question makes the result easier to assess and the next step easier to choose.

## Carry something forward

Keep one topic to read about and one thing to keep making. Leave a little room for whatever turns up along the way.

Over time, these small records become a trail worth returning to.
