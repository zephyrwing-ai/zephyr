A walk can be an exercise in noticing. Choose a familiar street, slow down, and look at the steps outside a shop, a bench beneath a tree, or a place where people tend to stop.

## Begin at a corner

A corner joins two directions. An entrance, a sign, and the turning movement of people meet in the same small space.

Describe three details. Consider how each one affects someone passing through.

## Notice the traces of use

A chair turned toward the shade, or a step used as a seat, records a choice someone made. A place's purpose also takes shape through daily use.

These traces make questions about space easier to understand.

## Keep a short description

Afterward, write a few sentences about the scene. A location, the light, and one specific action can be enough to bring the street back to mind.

Keeping these fragments is a way to practice paying closer attention to everyday life.
