To understand a room, begin with what happens there: sitting, passing through, pausing, looking outside. Its scale becomes meaningful through these ordinary actions.

## Pause at the entrance

The position of a door shapes the first view. A wall close ahead feels different from a long sightline toward a window.

Watch the paths around the entrance. Notice where someone might pause and how another person would move past.

![A room study showing the window, seating, and paths through the space.](/images/room-study.svg)

## Follow the light

A window brings time into a room. Across the day, the same table and wall take on different patterns of light and shade.

Light on a reading surface serves an immediate purpose. Light across a wall helps reveal the depth of the space.

### The view from a seat

Sit down and look outside. The frame of the window, a tree's shadow, and a distant building form a different composition at this height. Small details become easier to notice.

## Leave room for movement

The gaps between furniture leave room to turn, set down a book, or pull out a chair. These small allowances shape how comfortable a room feels in use.

Viewed through movement, sightlines, and light, a room becomes a place to experience.
